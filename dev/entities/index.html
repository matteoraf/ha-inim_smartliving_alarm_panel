    Entities¶

    This integration exposes your Inim alarm system's components as various Home Assistant entities. The entity IDs are programmatically generated based on the panel name and the names of your areas, zones, and scenarios fetched from the panel.

    • alarm_control_panel.your_panel_name: The main entity to arm and disarm the system. Its actions (Arm Home, Arm Away, etc.) are linked to the scenarios you mapped during setup. It shows Triggered while any area is in alarm. A scenario active on the panel that is not mapped to a mode shows as Armed custom bypass. Without a mapped Disarm scenario, the state is unknown when no mapping matches.

    • Areas:

      • switch.your_panel_name_area_X: Allows direct arming (on) and disarming (off) of an individual area. It is on when the area is armed in any mode (total, partial or instant); turning it on arms in the area's own mode set under Area switches arm mode per area, else in the mode of the Area switches default arm mode option (total by default). Attributes: status_text (armed, disarmed or unknown; Unknown when there is no status for the area) and arming_mode (total, partial, instant, disarmed or unknown).

      • select.your_panel_name_area_X_arming_mode (named Area X arming mode): The area's arming mode: Disarmed, Armed total, Armed partial or Armed instant (unknown while the panel reports none of these). Choosing a mode arms the area in that mode or disarms it; if the panel refuses, an error says why. Attributes: area_id and area_name.

        Its attributes also show the area's delays and options, as programmed on the panel: exit_time (time to leave after arming) and entry_time (time to disarm after a delayed zone opens), for example 120 s or 2 min; entry_time_2 (the second entry time, used by zones with the entry_time_2 option) or not used; pre_arm_warning_time and patrol_time (in minutes, for example 3 min); timer_program (the arm/disarm timer program, or none); and reset_alarm_memory_on_arming, auto_arm_partial and clear_call_queue_on_disarm (true or false). They are not shown for panels handled with another model's layout (see Known limitations).

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_area_X_alarm: On while the area is in alarm.

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_area_X_tamper: On while the area is in tamper.

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_area_X_triggered (named Area X Alarm memory): On after an alarm in the area until the alarm memory is reset (see Resetting the alarm memory). The tamper_memory attribute tells whether the area also has a tamper memory.

    • Zones:

      Only zones that exist on the panel get entities: zones of input, input/output and double terminals. Output terminals (for example a siren) and unused terminals have none. Zones 1 to N are the zones of the panel's N terminals (50 on the SmartLiving 1050, 100 on the 10100); a double terminal n also has a second zone, numbered N + n (51 to 100 on the 1050, 101 to 200 on the 10100).

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_zone_X: Represents a single zone. It is on only while the zone is in alarm (open); a short circuit or tamper shows in status_text (clear, alarmed, short or tamper) and in the zone's tamper sensor. test tells whether the zone is in test now.

        Device class: a wired zone does not tell the panel what detector it has, so the device class is a guess from the zone's programming: a shock sensor is vibration; a roller-blind sensor, a normally open or normally closed contact and a single EOL balancing are opening; a double EOL balancing is motion; anything else has no class. If the guess is wrong, change it in Home Assistant: open the entity's settings and pick Show as.

        Attributes: the zone's programming as read from the panel, with stable English values:

        • zone_id and zone_name: the zone's number and name.
        • zone_type: instant, delayed, viewable_delayed, route, 24_hour, automation, arm, disarm, switch, arm_disarm, patrol, emergency_exit_without_call or emergency_exit (a number if the panel uses a value not listed here).
        • alarm_cycles: how many times the zone can raise an alarm, or unlimited.
        • sensor_kind: generic, roller_blind or shock, from the terminal's programming. It decides the next attributes:
          • alarm_pulse_duration (generic sensors only), for example 300 ms or 20 s (the panel counts it in steps of 15 ms or of 10 s);
          • shock_sensitivity (shock sensors only), 0 to 40.
        • pulse_window_time, the time in which pulses are counted (for example 30 s or 1 min), and pulse_count, how many pulses in that time raise the alarm.
        • balancing: normally_open, normally_closed, single_eol, double_eol, double_zone, double_zone_eol or personalized.
        • assigned_areas, terminal_index, terminal_type (input, in_out, double, …), second_zone (the second zone of a double terminal) and internal_index.
        • The programming options, each true or false: interior, autobypass (bypassed by the panel when its areas arm while it is open), unbypassable, chime, test_programmed, autobypass_no_reinclude (autobypassed and not re-included when back at rest), no_arming_if_not_ready (stops arming in every mode, when the panel refuses arming with zones not ready), entry_time_2, last_exit_zone, reinclude_on_disarm, hold_up and fault_zone. Options that only apply to wireless zones are not shown.

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_zone_X_triggered (named Zone X Alarm memory): Turns on if the zone has triggered the system, until the alarm memory is reset. The tamper_memory attribute tells whether the zone's terminal has a tamper memory (both zones of a double terminal share it).

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_zone_X_tamper: Turns on while the zone is in tamper or short circuit (status_text tells which).

      • switch.your_panel_name_zone_X: Lets you enable or disable (exclude) individual zones, including the second zone of double terminals.

    • Scenarios:

      • button.your_panel_name_scenario_X: Allows direct activation of a specific scenario. If the activation fails (a zone not ready, or the panel refuses it), an error and a persistent notification with the reason appear in Home Assistant. The area_actions_details attribute tells what the scenario does to each area: keep, total, partial, instant or disarm. X is the scenario number minus 1 (scenario 1 is scenario_0), unlike area and zone numbers and the scenario field of activate_scenario.

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_scenario_X_active: A binary sensor that is 'on' if that specific scenario is currently the active one on the panel (X as for the buttons: the scenario number minus 1).

    • Sensors:

      • sensor.your_panel_name_event_log: The state is the position of the newest event in the panel's event log (0 to 999, or 0 to 499 on panels that keep 500 events: the SmartLiving 505 and 515, and the 1050 on firmware 5.1; it starts again from 0 when the log wraps around), so it changes with every new event. The attributes contain a human-readable log of recent events: authorized_by is the name of the user code or key, device the name of the keypad or reader (Remote for commands from the app, the cloud, Home Assistant or the installer software). Event times are the panel's clock read in Home Assistant's time zone, with daylight saving time.

      • sensor.your_panel_name_active_scenario: The active scenario's name followed by its index (0-based), for example Night (Index 2); the attribute active_scenario_index gives the index alone.

      • sensor.your_panel_name_firmware_version & sensor.your_panel_name_system_type: Provide static information about your alarm panel.

    • Outputs (only the ones you tick; see Outputs below):

      • switch.your_panel_name_output_name: An on/off or monostable output. It is on while the panel shows the output active (read at every poll). A monostable output switches itself off after its time; the switch shows it off at the next poll.

      • light.your_panel_name_output_name: A dimmer output, with brightness. On firmware 6 the level is read from the panel at every poll and after each change; on firmware 5.1 the panel does not report it, so the brightness is the last one set from Home Assistant.

      • Both are named with the output's name on the panel (Output n or O1 to O3 without one). Attributes: output_number, kind (on_off, monostable or dimmer), terminal_index or fixed_output (1 to 3), monostable_time (for example 30 s), home_automation (true if the installer marked it as a home automation output; not shown on firmware 5.1) and the output's options normally_closed, flashing, buzzer, keep_on_restoral, toggle and relay_use (true or false).

    • Maintenance:

      • button.your_panel_name_reread_panel_configuration: Reads the panel configuration again and reloads the integration (see Re-reading the panel configuration). Shown in the Configuration section of the device page.

      • sensor.your_panel_name_peripherals (named Peripherals): How many keypads, readers, expanders and sirens are enabled on the panel; the keypads, readers, expanders and sirens attributes list their names. It does not tell whether a device is answering. Shown in the Diagnostic section of the device page.

      • sensor.your_panel_name_user_code (named User code): The name of the user code the PIN belongs to (never the PIN); the number, areas (the areas it controls, by name) and type (standard, manager or master) attributes give more. Commands only work on those areas. Unknown if the PIN matches no user code: commands are refused then. Checked again whenever the integration reloads. Shown in the Diagnostic section of the device page.

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_refuses_arming_with_zones_not_ready (named Refuses arming with zones not ready): On when the installer set the panel to refuse arming with zones not ready (see Zones not ready). Read with the panel configuration; unknown until the configuration is read again by this version. Shown in the Diagnostic section of the device page.

      • binary_sensor.your_panel_name_programming_mode (named Programming mode): On while the panel is in programming mode (being programmed from a keypad, or reloading its configuration). Commands are not sent then. Shown in the Diagnostic section of the device page. It is unavailable for panels handled with the SmartLiving 1050's layout (other models, firmware 5.0x).

      • select.your_panel_name_diagnostics_level (named Diagnostics level): How much the next diagnostics download shows: Redacted, Full or Full confidential (see Sharing a system report). Disabled by default; it goes back to Redacted when the integration restarts or reloads, after the readable copy of a Full report has been downloaded, and 10 minutes after a Full report's first download. Shown in the Diagnostic section of the device page.

    (Replace your_panel_name, with actual value based on your setup).

    Outputs¶

    Warning

    An output switches whatever is wired to it: a siren, a lock, a gate, heating. Switching it from Home Assistant, or from an automation, acts on the real device. Before ticking an output, check what it drives; when you first try the integration's outputs, use one that is harmless to switch.

    • What is offered: outputs programmed on the panel as on/off, monostable or dimmer outputs (terminal outputs and O1 to O3) that your user code may switch. Analogue outputs are not offered. A terminal output on one of the panel's first three terminal positions cannot be switched remotely (the panel reserves those numbers for O1 to O3), so it is not listed.

    • What is ticked for you: only the outputs the installer marked as home automation outputs in the panel's programming (firmware 6; on firmware 5.1 the integration cannot read this mark, so nothing is ticked). The panel cannot tell what an output drives, so check them, and pick any other outputs yourself, in the setup or later in the options under Areas, zones, scenarios and outputs. The first time an existing installation shows the outputs in the options, the home automation outputs are ticked there too; after you save, your choice is kept.

    • Nothing is switched by itself: no output is switched at setup, at start, at a reload or when Home Assistant restores its state.

    • When the panel refuses: an output your user code may not switch, or one locked while its areas are armed, gives an error with the reason (see When the panel refuses a command). While the panel is in programming mode, no command is sent.
